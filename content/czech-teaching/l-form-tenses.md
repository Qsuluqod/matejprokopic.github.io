## The past tense

### The usage of l-form

The past tense uses the l-form in Czech. The l-form carries information about the grammatical number (singular/plural) and the gender of the doer of the action. Note that the l-form is the only form of a verb that carries the information about the gender. The information is carried by following endings:

| singular ||
|-|-|
|masculine (both) | **-ø**|
|feminine| **-a**|
|neuter| **-o** |

|plural ||
|-|-|
|masculine animate | **-i**|
|masculine inanimate | **-y** |
|feminine | **-y**|
|neuter | **-a** (inf. **-y**) |

To show them in context we demonstrate them on the verb **být - jsem - byl** (to be).

| singular ||
|-|-|
|masculine (both) | byl |
|feminine | byl**a** |
|neuter | byl**o** |

|plural ||
|-|-|
|masculine animate | byl**i**|
|masculine inanimate | byl**y** |
|feminine | byl**y**|
|neuter | byl**a** (inf. byl**y**) |

Note that since in the oral speech the **i** and **y** are pronounced **exactly** the same and since the informal form of neuter plural is very dominant in some reagions (such as Prague) the plural form of l-form is basically the same for all the genders (at least in the Prague oral production).

### Full past tense conjugation

Smart reader will notice that the information about the person is missing in the l-form, that means that this information needs to be conveyed in a different way. The answer to this issue is the auxilary verb which happens to be the verb **být - jsem - byl** (to be) in it's present tense. The complete past tense is showed bellow on the verb **dělat - dělám - dělal** (to do).

|past tense (masculine animate)||
|-|-|
|já **jsem** dělal| my **jsme** dělal**i**|
|ty **jsi** dělal| vy **jste** dělal**i**|
|on dělal| on**i** dělal**i**|

|past tense (masculine inanimate)||
|-|-|
|*já **jsem** dělal*| *my **jsme** dělal**y***|
|*ty **jsi** dělal*| *vy **jste** dělal**y***|
|on dělal| on**y** dělal**y**|

|past tense (feminine)||
|-|-|
|já **jsem** dělal**a**| my **jsme** dělal**y**|
|ty **jsi** dělal**a**| vy **jste** dělal**y**|
|on**a** dělal**a**| on**y** dělal**y**|

|past tense (neuter)||
|-|-|
|*já **jsem** dělal**o***| *my **jsme** dělal**a***|
|*ty **jsi** dělal**o***| *vy **jste** dělal**a***|
|on**o** dělal**o**| on**a** dělal**a** (inf. on**y** dělal**y**)|

Note that for the 3. person both singular and plural the auxiliary verb is missing. This is because the third person is usually the most used variant of the past tense. When there's something in the language that is the most used variant it can often get ommited and the speakers will recognize it's presence by it's absence (I know how it sounds). 

In Russian and many other languages the verb "to be" got completly ommited in the present tense. So instead of "I am student" speakers of Russian say just "I student" (we're now using pseudo russian-english). The "presence" of the verb "to be" is delivered through absence of any other verb. It works the same with the past tense, when you see l-form without any auxiliary verb, it must be 3. person.

Note that the form written in *italics* formally exist, but semantically they don't make a lot of sense semantically (the masculine inanimate and neuter are used mostly for object and objects usually don't speak about themselves in the 1. person singular), so they occur very rarely if ever.

Note that for 3. person we can profit from a little vowel harmony between the pronoun and the l-form, compare following:

- on**a** dělal**a**
- on**o** dělal**o**
- on**i** dělal**i**
- on**y** dělal**y**

### The auxiliary "to be"

The verb **být - jsem - byl** has it's informal form in the 2. person singular (**jseš**) that coexists with the more formal one (**jsi**). However when this verb is being used as auxiliary only the **jsi** form is allowed even in the utmost informal setting.

The verb **být - jsem - byl** used as auxiliary has also it's special position in the sentence. This won't be discussed here, since it's a broader topic but to give a little bit of an introduction, it always needs to be on the second position in the sentence, unless there is some other word (or words), that also needs to be on the second position, in this case they form a strict ordering in which they all pass to the second position.

### Oral speech curiosity

In Oral speech the auxiliary verb **být - jsem - byl** (to be) may be ommited as well in the 1. person both singular and plural. When it's ommited however the subject needs to be **expressed**. Look at following examples with verb **jít - jdu - š(e)l** (to go).

- já jsem tam šel -> já tam šel (I went there)
- my jsme tam šli -> my tam šli (We went there)

When you don't express the subject, due to the absence of the auxiliary verb, the meanings gets shifted to the 3. person, see:

- tam šel (he (unexpressed) went there)
- tam šli (they (unexpressed) went there)

Note that this happens because the subject can often be ommited very freely in Czech language.

## The conditional

The conditional uses the l-form together with a different tense of the auxiliary verb **být - jsem - byl**. This tense no longer exists in Czech and the only artefact we have from it is it's auxiliary usage in the conditional tense. The forms of the auxiliary verb are:

|||
|-|-|
|bych|bychom (inf. bysme)|
|bys | byste |
|by | by |

We show the full conditional conjugation on example with verb **chtít - chci - chtěl** (to want).

|masculine animate||
|-|-|
|já **bych** chtěl | my **bychom** chtěl**i** |
|ty **bys** chtěl | vy **byste** chtěl**i** |
|on **by** chtěl | oni **by** chtěl**i**|

|feminine||
|-|-|
|já **bych** chtěl**a** | my **bychom** chtěl**y**|
|ty **bys** chtěl**a** | vy **byste** chtěl**y**|
|on**a** **by** chtěl**a** | on**y** **by** chtěl**y**|

We haven't showed the tables for **masculine inanimate** and **neuter**, yet they are formed analogously and most of them don't make a lot of sense semantically as for the past tense.
